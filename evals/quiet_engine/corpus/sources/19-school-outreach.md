# School outreach

Lena Okafor teaches science at Port Alder High School. Since April her students have been part of the Harbor Weather Network, and the school field hosts the project's sixth weather station.

## What the students do

- Build sensor boards at the Tuesday build nights, supervised.
- Run sensor calibrations, with the reference thermometer, as a lesson in measurement error.
- Use the history dashboard for coursework: one group compared the temperature at the school field with the breakwater over a month and explained the difference with sea temperature.
- Keep the school field station's log: when it was cleaned, when the battery was checked.

## The school field station

The school field station is a standard weather station on a three-metre pole at the edge of the playing field, far enough from the buildings that they do not shelter it. It is the only station with a full sky view, so its rain gauge is the project's reference for rainfall. It uses the school's Wi-Fi.

## Lessons

Lena wrote up three lesson plans that other schools can reuse:

1. **What is a measurement?** Two uncalibrated sensors side by side; why they disagree; what an offset is.
2. **Weather from data.** Reading a pressure chart before and after a squall.
3. **Is it warmer by the sea?** Comparing stations, and the difference between correlation and cause.

## Safeguarding

Students never work at height and are never alone with adult volunteers; Lena or another teacher is always present. Student names are not published with their work unless the student and a parent agree.

## Results so far

Twelve students have helped build or calibrate stations. Two chose the weather station data for their final-year projects. The school wants a second station on the roof of the science block, which would let students study how buildings affect wind.

## Next steps

Ana Ruiz and Lena will apply to the town's community fund for a second school station and for a set of handheld anemometers for fieldwork.

## What the students taught us

The students found two things the volunteers had missed. One group noticed that the school field station's temperature rose every weekday at 10:30; the groundskeeper's mower was parked next to the pole during break. Another group showed that the cannery station's rain readings were half of the school field's on windy days, which led to the decision not to fit rain gauges on sheltered roofs.
