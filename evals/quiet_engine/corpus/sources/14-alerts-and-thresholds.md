# Alerts and thresholds

The sailing school asked for a warning before a squall reaches the bay, not a chart that shows it afterwards. This page sets out the storm warning: what it watches, the thresholds, and who gets it.

## What a squall looks like in our data

Looking back at the March capsize and two later squalls, the pattern at the breakwater was the same each time:

1. Pressure falls by more than 1.5 hPa in an hour.
2. The wind backs to the south-west.
3. Gusts climb from under 15 knots to over 25 knots within ten minutes.

The breakwater sees it first, typically eight to fifteen minutes before the sailing school.

## The storm warning

The storm warning fires when, at the breakwater or the lifeboat station:

- the gust exceeds 25 knots, **or**
- the pressure has fallen more than 1.5 hPa in the past hour **and** the gust exceeds 18 knots.

It clears when the gust has stayed below 15 knots for thirty minutes.

## Where the alert goes

- A banner on the waterfront overview dashboard in the boathouse.
- A message to the sailing school's duty phone.
- A message to the harbour office.

Alerts are generated by Grafana's alerting from the raw bucket, so they are no more than a minute behind the station.

## Anemometer health check

An alert that depends on one anemometer is only as good as that anemometer. A separate, quieter alert goes to Sam Patel and Tomas Weber when a station reports zero wind for six hours while its neighbours do not — the usual sign of a broken cup or a stuck reed switch.

## False alarms

In the first month the warning fired twice with no squall. Both times a gust on the breakwater alone crossed 25 knots for a single minute. We considered requiring two consecutive minutes, but the March squall built in under two minutes. We kept the single-minute rule and accept the occasional false alarm; the sailing school prefers a false alarm to a capsize.

## Battery alerts

A station whose battery voltage falls below 12.4 V triggers a maintenance alert to Priya Nair. It gives about two days of warning before the station goes dark.

## Thresholds are reviewed, not tuned in a hurry

Changing a threshold changes what the sailing school's duty instructor gets woken up for. A threshold change is proposed at the weekly sync with the history above, agreed with the sailing school, and only then changed in Grafana. The previous thresholds are kept on this page with the date they were replaced.

## What the warning is not

The storm warning is not a forecast and does not claim to be one. It says that the wind at the breakwater is doing something dangerous now. The wording of the message says exactly that, so nobody reads it as an all-clear when it is silent.
