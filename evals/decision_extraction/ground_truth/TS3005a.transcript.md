## Genuinely distinct subjects

**Count: 2.**

- Project | Remote control design project
- Event | Remote control project kick-off meeting

The **Project** is the spanning effort the whole transcript exists to launch:
a goal ("design a new remote control which has to be original, trendy and of
course, user friendly"), a method with a timespan (three phases — functional
design, conceptual design, detailed design, each split into individual work
and a meeting), financial targets from the finance department, and role
assignments carrying work past the end of the meeting. That is the rubric's
Project shape exactly: an ongoing effort defined by a goal and a timespan, not
a single bounded happening.

The **Event** is the meeting itself, and the rubric's own worked example is
directly on point: "a meeting transcript is fundamentally about the meeting
itself (an Event) and any Decisions reached — NOT about each of the five
participants named around the table." The document-vs-subject question — is
the meeting a subject here, or merely the medium? — was weighed and answered
*subject* for this file: a large share of the transcript is the meeting
conducting itself as a happening (the agenda walked in order, the room and
recording equipment introduced, the smart-board training, the
get-acquainted drawing exercise, the five-minute closing warning, the
scheduling of the next meeting in thirty minutes). The counter-reading — that
the transcript is only the carrier of the project content and the Event should
be dropped — was considered and rejected; it fails on the sheer proportion of
turns that are about running the meeting rather than about the remote control.

Three further candidates were examined for the subject list and rejected:

The AMI-annotated **production-cost decision** ("The production cost will be
12.50 Euro maximum") was weighed as a `Decision` subject and demoted to a
facet. The rubric's Decision is "ONE choice that was made — carrying its
rationale, the alternatives considered, and its current status — a
self-contained decision record". In this transcript the figure is announced,
not made: "from the finance department I have learned that... the production
cost will be twelve Euro fifty max", delivered in one breath with the selling
price, the profit aim and the international-market aim, with no rationale
stated and no alternative weighed by anyone in the room. A decision record
cannot be assembled from what this source carries. See Notes — this is the
most contestable call in the file.

The **three-phase design method** was weighed as a `Procedure` (a repeatable
how-to?) and rejected: it is two sentences of the project's own plan, never
developed as something to be performed again outside this project. It is the
Project's method facet.

The **smart-board how-to** (add a page, never delete one, change pen colour
and width) was weighed as a `Procedure` and rejected: it is incidental tool
training inside the meeting — an agenda item of the Event, not knowledge the
source is genuinely about.

The first five are the finance block, including the demoted AMI decision
anchor — held by the Project. The next three are agenda items of the Event
(the method briefing, the tool training, the get-acquainted exercise). The
frustrations/brainstorm family (buttons too many, remotes lost, boring, all
black; make it minimalist; put rare buttons behind protection; integrate
several remotes into one) is exploratory discussion that reaches no settled
idea and no decision — the PM's own framing is "maybe a minimalist design" and
"well maybe we should try to make it fun". A small choice-like moment inside
it — dropping Britain-specific TV features in favour of aiming at the whole
world ("we can leave that") — was examined as a `Decision` and rejected: no
record shape, barely a sentence of commitment. Role assignments (Marketing
Expert → user requirement specification, Interface Designer → technical
functions design, Industrial Designer → working design) are the closing facet
of the Event/Project boundary.

This list is drawn from the document's structure; the 2026-08-08 queue added
the last two observed spellings (`User-Friendly Design`, a fragment of the
project's stated goal; `Remote Control Design Specifications`, a shredded
attribute of the Project, not a subject).

Revisited in the first adjudication pass (2026-08-07, sweep of 5 runs × 2
pipelines): `AMI meeting TS3005a` was the SOLE title in all 10 runs — the
production-title collapse now tracked in
[#459](https://github.com/jasonssdev/openkos/issues/459). The call above

genuine Event *is* roughly what the source title names, so suppression must
not zero out a run whose only genuine subject is the specific meeting.

**Person/Organization/Place — settling the report's open question for this
file.** The report counts 17 annotated Person mentions, 4 Organization and 3
Place against zero emitted objects and asks whether that is a defect. Against
this source it is not. Every speaker is anonymized (`A:`–`D:`) and every
personal name is elided mid-sentence by the corpus itself ("My name is and uh
I hope you will introduce yourself"); the rubric's Person requires ONE
specific, NAMED individual, so no Person subject is reachable from this
transcript — the annotated mentions are pronouns and role words, not names.
The roles themselves (Project Manager, User Interface Design, Industrial
Designer, Marketing Expert) are functions, not named people. The employer and
its finance department are never named, so no Organization is reachable
either. The only named place-like mentions (Britain) and the only named human
(Bob Ross, a joke) are passing mentions filed out of scope. Zero
Person/Organization/Place emissions on this source are correct behaviour under
the rubric, not extraction misses.

**Most contestable call: the demoted production-cost decision.** AMI's own
annotators wrote exactly one decision sentence for this meeting and this draft
files it as a facet, on the grounds that the transcript announces the
constraint rather than deliberating it (no rationale, no alternatives, no
decision moment — the rubric's record shape is absent). A reviewer who reads
AMI's annotation as overriding the record-shape requirement would promote
`Decision | Production cost cap of 12.50 Euro` and move this file to Count: 3.
The tension is real and is left to the human adjudication pass.

**Secondary tension: the Event call.** If a later pass adopts a stricter
document-vs-subject line for meeting transcripts (the transcript as mere
medium), the Event drops and this file becomes Count: 1 (the Project alone).
This draft keeps the Event because the rubric's own meeting example instructs
it and the meeting's self-conduct owns a large share of the turns.
