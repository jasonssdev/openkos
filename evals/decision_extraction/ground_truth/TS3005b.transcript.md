## Genuinely distinct subjects

**Count: 7.**

- Event | Remote control functional design meeting
- Project | Remote control design project
- Concept | Working design of an infrared remote control
- Decision | Button-less LCD touch screen remote
- Decision | Remote control function set
- Decision | Remote control power management
- Decision | Speech recognition only if budget remains

The **Event** is the meeting itself, per the rubric's own worked example ("a
meeting transcript is fundamentally about the meeting itself (an Event) and
any Decisions reached"). The document-vs-subject question was weighed and
answered *subject*, as in `TS3005a.transcript.md`: the transcript develops the
meeting as a happening — the agenda walked in order, the PM doubling as
secretary and promising minutes into the project folder, three prepared
presentations delivered in turn, the five-minute signal, the closing hand-off
to lunch and the next meeting. The counter-reading (transcript as mere medium)
loses to that self-conduct, though less decisively than in `TS3005a`, where
the meeting's own mechanics dominate; here the content dominates, and a
reviewer taking the stricter line would drop this Event to Count: 6.

The **Project** is the same spanning effort as in `TS3005a.transcript.md`,
developed here through its second phase: the 12.50-Euro production ceiling
constrains three separate arguments, the new management requirements re-aim
the product (under-forty market, television only, corporate image), and the
closing schedules the next share of individual work. An ongoing effort with a
goal and a timespan, distinct from any one meeting.

The **Concept** is the Industrial Designer's working-design presentation: how
a remote control physically works — button presses a print plate, the chip
senses the connection, composes a Morse-code-like signal, transistors drive an
infra-red LED, the television's sensor receives it — developed over many turns
with a diagram/animation, plus manufacturing findings (as easy to mass-produce
as printing a page; the technology already exists). This explains a mechanism,
so it is `Concept`, not `Procedure` (nothing here is a how-to meant to be
performed); that was the tie-break weighed. It is also not a facet: it is a
self-contained explanation that would survive extraction from this transcript
with no reference to any decision.

The four **Decisions** group AMI's fifteen decision sentences into
self-contained decision records — choice, rationale, alternatives, status —
where the transcript actually carries that shape. The mapping, by line number
of `TS3005b.decisions.txt`:

**`Button-less LCD touch screen remote` (lines 6, 15).** The choice: the
remote's interface is an LCD touch screen, and consequently the remote has no
physical buttons at all — the PM names it explicitly ("So actually we're going
to create a button-less remote. No buttons at all... that might be a unique
selling point"). Rationale on the record: the under-forty target market's
measured interest in LCD screens (81% of 16–25, 66% of 26–35, 55% of 36–45),
durability over print plates, the fancy-factor (80% would spend more on a
fancy-looking remote). Alternatives weighed on the record: rubber buttons
(kept as the explicit backup plan if the LCD proves too expensive — "if it
costs gets too much... we should be sticking to rubber buttons"), and a
physical on-button (debated at length, resolved by tap-to-wake). Status:
adopted, contingent on cost ("if we can afford it"). This is the meeting's
central decision and the richest record in the file.

**`Remote control function set` (lines 5, 7, 13, 14; line 1 partially).** The
choice: what is on the remote — power, volume selection, channel selection,
digits zero to nine, a two-digit switch button, mute, a battery indicator, a
general menu with the most used functions, separate sub-menus for teletext,
screen settings, audio settings and channel settings, and the possibility to
enlarge buttons for elderly users. The PM reads this list back twice as the
taken decision — it is the agenda item the meeting existed for ("we have to
take a decision on the remote control functions"). Rationale: the marketing
findings (volume and channel are the most-used buttons; 75% zap a lot;
elderly users need large targets). Alternatives: full teletext support
(demoted to a sub-menu because "teletext has become outdated" — AMI's line 1
lands here in its softened, decided form), play/pause and video functions
(rejected with television-only scope). Status: adopted. Kept separate from
the LCD decision because they answer different questions — which interface
technology versus which function inventory — and the transcript decides them
in separate passes; see Notes for the counter-reading.

**`Remote control power management` (lines 8–11).** The choice: a
rechargeable battery with a charger/docking station, the remote shutting down
when the television shuts down, automatic standby after two–three idle
minutes, and a low-battery indicator on screen giving time to dock it. This
is one deliberative arc, not four: it opens from the LCD's power draw
("you run it on batteries, the batteries will be empty very fast"), weighs
disposable batteries against a recharger (you forget to dock it — but you
also forget to buy batteries; docking means you cannot lose it anymore),
rejects a manual on/off switch for the remote, and settles the whole bundle.
Rationale, alternatives and status are all on the record. Grouping these four
AMI sentences into one record instead of four subjects is exactly the
fragment-demotion this pass is for.

**`Speech recognition only if budget remains` (line 12).** The choice: speech
recognition ships only if money is left in the budget. Rationale: it polls
even higher than the LCD (91% of 16–25s would pay more for it) but nobody has
cost figures, and international sale means supporting many languages and
dialects, which the team doubts is feasible at 12.50. Alternatives: implement
it outright (rejected on cost), drop it entirely (rejected on the market
numbers). Status: deferred-conditional — "we only do this when we have enough
money left." Small but a complete record, and the transcript treats it as its
own agenda point.

AMI lines 1–4 — the four new project requirements (pay no attention to
teletext; television only; reach a market under forty; corporate image
recognizable) — are deliberately NOT a Decision subject. They are management
directives read to the team from the PM's laptop, made elsewhere: the meeting
receives them, it does not make them, and no alternatives are weighed (the
one flicker of deliberation, "the video also, or not?", is answered by
pointing at the requirement). They are filed as facets of the Project, whose
re-aiming they are. See Notes; this demotion is contestable, since four of
AMI's fifteen sentences land there.

One further candidate was examined for the subject list and rejected: the
Marketing Expert's **user-requirements presentation** (the market
percentages, the lose-it/ugly/zap findings). It has real development, but
every number in it exists in this transcript as the rationale material of the
decisions above — it is where the LCD and speech-recognition records get
their evidence, not a subject the source is about in its own right. Demoted
to a facet; flagged in Notes. The Interface Designer's technical-functions
presentation was examined the same way and demoted more easily: it is thinner
and flows directly into the function-set discussion.

Fragments and rationale material of the subjects above, including every AMI
decision sentence demoted rather than grouped. An extractor emitting these as
standalone objects is shredding a decision record or the project into
attributes.

- Separate teletext menu
- Screen, audio and channel settings sub-menus
- General menu with most used functions
- Enlargeable buttons
- Low battery indicator
- Standby mode
- Shutdown with the television
- Docking station
- Rubber buttons backup plan
- Speech recognition market percentages
- Adjustable fronts

The first five are the management-requirements block (AMI lines 1–4), held by
the Project. The presentation family holds the two demoted presentations and
their framing. The button/menu family are components of the
`Remote control function set` record; the power family (low battery indicator
through rubber-buttons backup) are components of `Remote control power
management` and the LCD record's contingency; the last three are late
brainstorm items (fronts like phones, colour variants) that reach no
decision. This list is drawn from the document's structure, not yet from
measured runs; the adjudication queue will extend it with observed spellings.

## Notes

Person policy: see `examples/extraction-corpus/annotation-guidelines.md` §2 —

**What this settles about the `5, 1, 7` judge run.** `report.md` records a
union+judge run on this source where the judge kept 1 of roughly 10 merged
candidates, and #457 exists because nobody could say whether those rejections
were facets (right) or subjects (wrong). Against this draft the answer is:
with 7 genuine subjects here, any run keeping 1 object discarded genuine
material — the collapse silhouette is a real defect on this source, produced
by selection, whatever the discarded titles turn out to have been. The blind
cap's stable 6 was plausibly close to right in COUNT; whether it was right in
CONTENT needs its titles adjudicated against this list.

**Twin-rule probe.** `derive_source_title` yields `'AMI meeting TS3005b'`.
As in the `TS3005a` file, an Event carrying exactly that umbrella title is
the summarising-collapse silhouette (this source produced a single Event in
all three baseline runs), and it is deliberately not aliased to the genuine
Event subject in this draft: crediting the umbrella title would score
collapse as recovery. The anti-twin floor case cuts the other way too — a
transcript's one genuine Event is roughly what the source's own title names,
so twin suppression must not zero a run whose only emission is the specific
meeting. Both edges should be revisited with runs in hand.

**Person/Organization/Place.** The report counts 3 annotated Person mentions
and 1 Organization against zero emissions. Against this source, zero is
correct under the rubric: the only personal name that survives anonymization
is `Dennie`, a passing spelling question (out of scope above); every other
person-mention is a pronoun or a role (Project Manager, Industrial Designer,
Marketing Expert). The employer is never named — it appears only as "the
company" behind the corporate-image requirement — so no Organization subject
is reachable. No place is named at all. Together with the `TS3005a` file this
settles the report's participant-extraction open question for both
transcripts: the absences are correct behaviour, not misses.

**Contestable calls, in descending order of doubt.** (1) The
management-requirements demotion: four of AMI's fifteen decision sentences
are filed as Project facets on made-elsewhere/no-deliberation grounds; a
reviewer who reads "decision reached the team" more loosely would promote one
`Decision | New project requirements` and move this file to Count: 8. (2) The
LCD/function-set boundary: a stricter lumper could merge the two into one
remote-control-design decision record (Count: 6); this draft keeps them
apart because the transcript decides them separately and AMI's own sentences
split the same way. (3) The user-requirements presentation: it owns real
transcript share and a promoted `Concept | Remote control user requirements`
is defensible; demoted here because its content functions as rationale inside
the decision records. (4) The power-management grouping bundles four AMI
sentences into one record; a splitter could break out the recharger choice
(the only piece with its own weighed alternatives) and facet the rest.
